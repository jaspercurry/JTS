## The S0–S4 spike rig (what every number below was produced on)

§10.2 S0 specifies a spike, not a product bond: "Point a snapclient at
`jts_ring_grouping` with a live reader attached." That is exactly what was
built, on jts.local, touching neither the product's snapcast units, its
snapfifo, nor its DAC:

- **server** — a standalone `snapserver` on the default ports (the product's
  units are stopped), mirroring `snapserver_argv` byte-for-byte in shape:
  `--stream.source "pipe:///tmp/hwpass/fifo?name=hwpass&mode=create&sampleformat=48000:16:2&codec=flac" --stream.buffer 400`
- **source** — `sox -n -t raw -r 48000 -e signed -b 16 -c 2 - synth … sine 440 sine 660 vol 0.2 > fifo`
- **writer** — a real `snapclient --host 127.0.0.1 --latency 0 --soundcard
  jts_ring_grouping --player alsa` (`--latency 0` is
  `DEFAULT_CLIENT_LATENCY_MS`, `jasper/multiroom/config.py:83`)
- **reader** — a real `CamillaDSP 4.1.3` capturing `jts_ring_grouping`
  (S16_LE/2ch/48000, `chunksize: 128`, `enable_rate_adjust: true`, no
  resampler), or `arecord` on the same PCM

**Rig caveat, stated because it is load-bearing.** The reader's config is
hand-written for this spike, not the product's generated endpoint config
(`build_baseline_profile_candidate(driver_domain=True, …)`), which cannot be
produced on a passive box. Where a result depends on the reader, that is said.
Results that depend only on the writer do not carry this caveat.

**Instrument verification, before any null was trusted.** Three controls on the
identical server/stream/client:

| Control | Transport | `Failed to get chunk` in 20 s | buffer median | iterations/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | `--player file:filename=/dev/null` | **0** | **0 ms** | 347 |
| **B** | `--soundcard null --player alsa` | 133 | −799 ms | 1 |
| **C** | `--soundcard hw:Loopback,0,0 --player alsa` + `arecord` drain | **1** (startup) | **0 ms** | 500 |

Control A proves the server, the sox feed, the stream and snapclient are
healthy. Control C proves snapclient's **ALSA player** locks correctly on this
box, on the pre-flip transport, at `--latency 0`. Control B is a `null`-device
artefact (it never consumes at rate) and is reported only so the reader knows
it was run and discounted. **A null result on the ring is therefore
attributable, not ambient.**

## S0 — does snapclient negotiate and run against the ioplug? — **NEGOTIATION PASS / RUN FAIL**

### The negotiation half — PASS, against §10.2 S0's bar verbatim

```
$ snapclient --host 127.0.0.1 --latency 0 --soundcard jts_ring_grouping \
             --player alsa --logsink stderr --logfilter '*:debug'
[Info] (Alsa) Period time too large, changing from 20000 to 2667
[Info] (Alsa) PCM name: jts_ring_grouping, sample rate: 48000 Hz, channels: 2,
              buffer time: 42666 us, periods: 16, period time: 2666 us,
              period frames: 128
```

Against the bar, item by item: `sample rate: 48000 Hz` ✓, `channels: 2` ✓,
`periods: 16` ✓, `period frames: 128` ✓, the `Period time too large, changing
from 20000 to 2667` line present and benign ✓. **Two values read 1 µs below
the bar's text** — `buffer time: 42666` vs the design's `42667`, `period time:
2666` vs `2667`. 2048/48000 s = 42666.67 µs and 128/48000 s = 2666.67 µs; the
design rounded up, snapclient truncates. Same geometry, different rounding —
recorded rather than glossed.

Absent, as required: no `snd_pcm_avail_delay failed: … bad state (-77)` (the
snapcast#1154 shape), no `Can't set …`, no `Can't write to PCM device`, no
`-EBADFD`, no repeated re-init. The ioplug's capture side negotiates equally
cleanly — CamillaDSP's own probe of the same PCM:

```
Capture: supported channels, min: 2, max: 2, list: [2]
Capture: supported samplerates: Discrete([48000])
Capture: supported sample formats: [S16_LE]
Minimum period is 128 frames, buffer size is minimum 512 frames
```

### The run half — FAIL

snapclient negotiates and then never achieves clock lock. Steady state, reader
attached, 60 s:

```
[Debug] (Stream) Silent frames: 96, frames: 128, age: -2.001
[Info]  (Stream) pMiniBuffer->full() && (abs(pMiniBuffer->mean()) > 50): -52000
[Debug] (Stats) Chunk: -20   -20   -20   -20   1   40   0
```

- `pMiniBuffer->full() && (abs(pMiniBuffer->mean()) > 50)` is snapclient's
  **hard-sync trigger**, and it fires **615 times in 60 s (~10 Hz)**,
  continuously, without converging. §10.2 S3's PASS bar is **zero** hard syncs
  after a 60 s settle; the `miniBuffer_ > 50 ms` signal it names is precisely
  this line.
- The buffer median oscillates between −20 ms and −160 ms and never reaches 0.
  Controls A and C both sit at **0 ms**.
- The 5th `Chunk:` column — successful play iterations in the stats second — is
  **1** on the ring against **500** (aloop) and **347** (file).

### Attribution — five configurations, all measured

| # | Configuration | `Failed to get chunk` | hard-sync triggers | buffer median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ring, no reader | 75 / 25 s | — | −420 ms |
| 2 | ring, `arecord` reader | 75 / 25 s | 0 | −420 ms |
| 3 | ring, CamillaDSP reader (File sink), writer first | many | ~10 Hz | −19 … −25 ms |
| 4 | ring, CamillaDSP reader (clocked sink), **reader first** | **1** (startup) | **615 / 60 s** | −20 … −160 ms |
| 5 | `hw:Loopback,0,0`, `arecord` reader (Control C) | 1 (startup) | 0 | **0 ms** |

Configuration 4 is the important one: with the reader up **before** the writer
there is no readerless window at all, and snapclient still hard-syncs at ~10 Hz
forever.

**Geometry is not the cause.** Two scratch ring PCMs were declared (a temporary
`/etc/alsa/conf.d/99-hwpass-scratch.conf`, since removed — see Lab changes) and
driven by the same client:

| PCM | negotiated buffer | `Failed to get chunk` / 25 s | buffer median |
|---|---|---|---|
| `jts_ring_grouping` (shipped, 128×16) | 42 666 us | 75 | −420 ms |
| scratch 256×16 (**§3.2's named fallback**) | 85 333 us | 84 | −840 ms |

### The mechanism — measured

`snd_pcm_avail_delay()` sampled from a blocking `snd_pcm_writei` writer
(`/tmp/hwpass/probe.py`, ctypes on libasound, one sample per 128-frame write):

| Reader | frames written / s | nominal | ratio | `delay` med | `avail` min |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none (free-run) | 38 095 766 | 48 000 | **794×** | 2048 fr (42.667 ms) | 2048 |
| `arecord` | 3 060 990 | 48 000 | **63.8×** | 1024 fr (21.333 ms) | 0 |
| CamillaDSP (clocked sink) | 213 162 | 48 000 | **4.44×** | 2048 fr (42.667 ms) | 0 |
| **`hw:Loopback,0,0` + `arecord`** | **48 190** | 48 000 | **1.004×** | 4212 fr (87.75 ms) | 0 |

**`snd_pcm_writei()` on the grouping ring does not pace a blocking writer.** On
snd-aloop the same probe is held to 1.004× nominal by the device; on the
grouping ring it runs 4×–794× real time depending only on how fast the reader
drains. This is the ioplug behaving as designed — `pcm_jts_ring.c`'s dual-mode
`avail` deliberately never gates the writer, which is B1's anti-wedge fix — but
snapclient's ALSA player is a consumer that **requires** the device to pace it
(Control C is the proof that it locks when a device does). Nothing else in the
rig supplies a clock, so snapclient free-runs and hard-syncs forever.

The ioplug's own `.delay` comment (`pcm_jts_ring.c`, `jts_ring_delay`)
anticipated snapclient reading this callback and predicted: *"snapclient's own
gate is bufferMs 400 …, an order of magnitude away, so it keeps emitting chunks
throughout; the expected effect is ONE hard sync when the reader attaches."*
**That prediction is falsified.** The measured delay does saturate exactly where
the comment says (2048 frames = 42.667 ms, never above), so the *delay* half of
the reasoning holds — but the consequence drawn from it does not: snapclient
does not keep emitting, and there is not one hard sync but a permanent ~10 Hz
storm, in every start order and at every geometry tried.

The brief names this exact outcome as an escalation trigger: *"a non-saturating
delay, or repeated hard syncs = STOP and escalate (it falsifies the
free-run-drop assumption; the `.delay` dead-mode discount becomes owed as a
code change)."* The delay **does** saturate; the repeated hard syncs are
present. **Stopping and escalating.**

# Addendum — 2026-08-20, later: the DAC-in-loop experiment (Leg A / Leg B)

One bounded experiment, mandated before any governor code is written. **No
product code was changed.** jts.local only, still on `648544c38`.

**The gap this closes.** Every configuration in the S0 pass above had *no
hardware clock in the loop* — the reader was `arecord` (unclocked), a
`File`-sink CamillaDSP (unclocked), or a CamillaDSP whose sink was snd-aloop (a
software timer). Leg A puts a real crystal in the chain and asks whether DAC
back-pressure paces it **without a governor**; Leg B asks whether a stalled
reader storms.

## Rig — how the crystal got into the loop

`jasper-outputd` was the **sole** holder of the Apple dongle:

```
$ sudo fuser -v /dev/snd/pcmC3D0p

```

and it is `Restart=on-failure` with no `PartOf`/`BindsTo`, so a **clean stop**
frees the DAC without cascading and without arming
`jasper-camilla-recover` (which is an `OnFailure=` hook, not a stop hook).
Stopping outputd alone was therefore the whole intervention — `jasper-camilla`
and `jasper-fanin` stayed up and free-ran into `jts_ring_playback`, which is
the ring's designed readerless behaviour.

The dongle is a genuine hardware clock:

Spike reader (`/tmp/hwpass/legA-reader.yml`), capture = the ring, playback =
that DAC:

### `writer_drop_no_reader` per graded window — derived, then validated

`writer_drop_no_reader` lives in the process-local `jts_ring_writer_t`
(`c/jts-ring-ioplug/jts_ring_shm.h:277`), **not** in the SHM header, so it is
not externally readable per window. It is derived instead: on the free-run path
the writer "advance[s] `read_seq` on the [reader's] behalf" (`jts_ring_shm.h:386`),
so a drop moves **both** seqs — per-window drops are the `read_seq` advance
accrued while the reader's heartbeat was not advancing.

`reader_pid` is **not** a liveness signal and was not used: it never read 0 in
this run (values seen: `202083`, `207648` — the original and post-restart
camilla). Liveness is `reader_heartbeat_ns` advancing between samples.

**Validation:** derived total **11 919** vs the authoritative close-time
`drop_no_reader` **11 735** — **101.6 % agreement**, the 1.6 % being
heartbeat-sampling granularity at window edges. The derivation therefore has
provenance and the per-window numbers stand.

### Probe source, committed to the record

```python

"""Instrumented grouping-ring writer probe (Addendum 4).

Opens jts_ring_grouping as a BLOCKING playback writer, dumps the negotiated
hw_params AND the full sw_params (start_threshold especially), then writes
silence period-by-period, sampling on every write:

    t, avail, delay, write_seq, read_seq, reader_pid, reader_heartbeat_ns

write_seq/read_seq/reader_* come from the ring's 128-byte SHM header, mmapped
read-only (offsets pinned by _Static_assert in c/jts-ring-ioplug/jts_ring_shm.h:
writer_epoch@32, write_seq@40, read_seq@48, writer_pid@56,
writer_heartbeat_ns@64, reader_pid@72, reader_heartbeat_ns@80).

WHY the header and not the counter: writer_drop_no_reader lives in the
process-local jts_ring_writer_t (jts_ring_shm.h:277), not in SHM, so it is not
externally readable per window. On the free-run path the writer "advance[s]
read_seq on the [reader's] behalf" (jts_ring_shm.h:386), so a drop moves BOTH
seqs. Per-window drops are therefore derived as the read_seq advance that
happened while no live reader was attached, and the derivation is validated
against the authoritative close-time drop_no_reader for the whole run.

usage: ring_probe_v2.py <pcm> <seconds> <outfile>
"""
import ctypes
import ctypes.util
import mmap
import os
import struct
import sys
import time

SND_PCM_STREAM_PLAYBACK = 0
SND_PCM_FORMAT_S16_LE = 2
SND_PCM_ACCESS_RW_INTERLEAVED = 3
RING_PATH = "/dev/shm/jts-ring/grouping.ring"
HDR = {"writer_epoch": 32, "write_seq": 40, "read_seq": 48,
       "writer_pid": 56, "writer_heartbeat_ns": 64,
       "reader_pid": 72, "reader_heartbeat_ns": 80}

# … (parameter-dump helpers elided; full file below in the write-loop) …
    written, errors = 0, {}
    fh = open(outfile, "w")
    t0 = time.monotonic()
    t_end = t0 + seconds
    while time.monotonic() < t_end:
        rc = lib.snd_pcm_writei(handle, buf, period)
        if rc < 0:
            errors[rc] = errors.get(rc, 0) + 1
            lib.snd_pcm_recover(handle, ctypes.c_int(rc), 1)
            continue
        written += rc
        lib.snd_pcm_avail_delay(handle, ctypes.byref(availp), ctypes.byref(delayp))
        fh.write("%.6f\t%d\t%d\t%d\t%d\t%d\t%d\n" % (
            time.monotonic() - t0, availp.value, delayp.value,
            h("write_seq"), h("read_seq"), h("reader_pid"),
            h("reader_heartbeat_ns")))
    elapsed = time.monotonic() - t0
    fh.close()
    lib.snd_pcm_close(handle)   # prints the authoritative close-time counters
    hdr.close(); os.close(fd)
    print(f"  elapsed={elapsed:.2f}s frames_written={written} "
          f"implied={written/elapsed:.1f} fr/s errors={errors or 'none'}")
```

### Pace ledger — the new cumulative+delta format

```
bind    bound_s= 0.000 (+ 0.000 this bind)    cold start (open)
release bound_s= 0.002 (+ 0.002 this bind)    2 ms later
                                              [t=120 SIGSTOP]

                                              [t=150 SIGCONT]

                                              [t=270 SIGKILL]

                                              [t=275 restart]
```

- **Cumulative+delta readable without differencing — PASS.** Every release
  states its own bind's duration (`+0.002`, `+28.018`, `+3.080`); no two lines
  had to be subtracted.
- **No phantom release, no absurd delta across camilla's restart — PASS.** The
  cumulative is monotonic (0.000 → 0.002 → 44.343 → 72.362 → 81.009 → 84.089)
  and every delta is sane. The SIGKILL bind/release pair brackets the restart
  cleanly.
- **Exactly ONE bind at the stall, in the new format — PASS.**
- **Release ≤ ~2 s after SIGCONT — PASS.** The stall ran t=120→150 (30 s) and
  the bind's own delta is 28.018 s, so the bind engaged ≈2 s after SIGSTOP and
  released essentially **at** SIGCONT. Reading that from one line is exactly
  what the delta format bought.

### The bar that is still missed, and what the counter reveals

**Bar: ZERO pace lines through the whole locked phase. Observed: 2** — the
`bind`/`release` pair at open, spanning **2 ms** (previous build: 3 ms;
original: 57.057 s). After that pair, **zero logged edges** until the stall
bind at t=120.

But the cumulative counter says the log is **not a sufficient statistic for
inertness**: `bound_s` advanced from **0.002 to 44.343 — +44.341 s of bound
time — across the locked phase with zero logged edges in that interval.** The
logger window-suppresses edges by design (`pcm_jts_ring.c:312-313`: an edge
"whose predecessor was window-suppressed can only be sized by differencing two
lines that may be an hour apart"), which is precisely why the delta field was
added.

That +44.341 s lines up with the cold-start ramp measured independently below
(full lock at **+42.18 s**): the governor was the limiter for essentially the
whole pre-lock ramp, then went quiet. Coherent — and it means "zero pace lines"
could have been satisfied by a build that binds heavily but logs nothing.
**Recorded, not adjudicated.**
